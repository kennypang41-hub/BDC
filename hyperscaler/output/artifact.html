The commitments that never reach the balance sheet

Six hyperscalers have signed leases and purchase agreements that bind future cash but sit in the footnotes rather than on the balance sheet. This tracks both lines — leases signed but not yet commenced, and non-cancelable purchase commitments — quarter by quarter, as each company discloses them.

Alphabet · Microsoft · Meta · Amazon · NVIDIA · Oracle USD billions

Under ASC 842 a signed lease becomes a liability only when the lessor hands over the asset. Until then the whole obligation lives in a footnote — which is why these totals can grow by a hundred billion dollars in a quarter without moving a reported liability.

Checking the total against Morgan Stanley

  • Four of six agree independently. Alphabet, Meta, Amazon and Oracle each land within a billion of the published chart, having been sourced from the filings rather than from it. Microsoft matches trivially — its figure for this quarter is the chart's, so it corroborates nothing.
  • The gap is entirely NVIDIA. This dataset carries the $119bn disclosed for the quarter ended 26 April 2026; the chart used $155bn, most likely NVIDIA's January fiscal year-end on a broader definition that folds in other purchase obligations and cloud service agreements.
  • Definitions are not uniform. Alphabet reports “purchase commitments and other contractual obligations”, Amazon “unconditional purchase obligations”, Meta “non-cancelable contractual commitments”. Cross-company totals are indicative, not like-for-like.

Every figure, with its provenance

Each observation carries how it was sourced. filing and filing_table come from the filing's own footnote or maturity table; press and derived are weaker and are flagged wherever they appear.

Method, and what this cannot tell you

  • Coverage is 59 of a possible 144 cells. 2023 and much of 2024 are thin by nature: several of these companies only began disclosing a material uncommenced-lease balance during 2025, and NVIDIA does not disclose one at all — its exposure sits in supply and capacity purchase obligations instead. An empty cell means no disclosure was sourced. It is never a zero and never an estimate.
  • Fiscal calendars differ. Quarters are calendar-aligned for comparability. Microsoft's year ends in June, NVIDIA's in late January, Oracle's in May, so their figures sit at the nearest calendar quarter — the exact balance-sheet date is on every row of the table and in the workbook.
  • The two measures overlap in spirit, not in accounting. Adding a company's leases to its purchase commitments gives a sense of total forward obligation, but the two are disclosed under different standards and neither is a debt figure.
  • Sourcing constraint. SEC.gov and every filing mirror were unreachable from the environment this was built in, so figures were collected by searching filing text rather than parsing EDGAR directly. Where two sources disagreed, maturity tables that sum to their stated total were preferred, and the Morgan Stanley cross-check above was used as an independent test.
